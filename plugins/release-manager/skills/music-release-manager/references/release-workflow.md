# Music release workflow

Use this as a reusable operating guide. It gives decision rules, not permission to make external changes.

## 1. Intake and single source of truth

Create or refresh a release record from current files and direct user confirmation. Keep these groups separate:

| Group | Capture | Verify before external use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | artist, exact title, version, release type, date, label, distributor | title and date on distributor and public artwork |
| IDs and rights | UPC, ISRC, writers, producers, performers, splits, master owner, copyright, territories | contributor roles, ownership and restrictions |
| Assets | final master, cover, vertical/social versions, press images, photo credits | file format, dimensions, media permission |
| Public path | artist profile, pre-save/smart link, final DSP URLs, social accounts | every destination works and belongs to this release |
| Activity ledger | completed, scheduled, drafted, sent, live, awaiting reply | platform UI, calendar, Sent mail, or tracker |

Never turn a working inference into release metadata. Do not reuse an old release's identifiers, dates, budget, or claims.

## 2. Files, metadata and delivery

- Inspect the final master rather than trusting a filename. Record duration, format, sample rate/bit depth, and any delivery validation result.
- Confirm final artwork is distributor-compliant and has cleared rights. Treat photographer and artwork credits as rights information, not decorative copy.
- Compare title, artist, date, genre, language, explicit status, credits, codes, and copyright lines across the distributor, press kit, smart link and social copy.
- Use genre and culture fields precisely. A multilingual lyric or an artist's heritage is not evidence for a regional genre label. Choose only categories that the arrangement and the platform options support.
- Keep delivery stages separate: `draft`, `submitted`, `delivered`, `product page exists`, and `live`. Product pages and public links may appear at different times.
- Do not submit or alter a distributor release without explicit approval of the final action.

## 3. Lyrics and editorial setup

- Transcribe or use a supplied lyric sheet, then check it against the final recording. Preserve intentional multilingual wording; flag uncertainty instead of silently "fixing" it.
- Confirm languages and explicit status for the complete recording, including ad-libs, before supplying broadcast or lyric-platform metadata.
- For Spotify editorial pitching, submit while the track is unreleased and use accurate story, genre, moods, instruments, cultures, and planned promotion. Do not claim editorial placement is likely or guaranteed.
- For Spotify Canvas, check the live platform requirements before upload. Verify that the resulting asset is accepted and visibly attached. Do not click final submission if the user asks only to prepare it.
- For lyric services such as Musixmatch, distinguish publishing/transcription, lyric timing, and an actual platform display. A paid plan or a submission does not guarantee propagation by release day.

## 4. Smart links, profiles and organic content

- Create a pre-save/smart link only after confirming the title, artwork and Spotify destination. Test it in a clean view.
- Before release, use one primary CTA: **Pre-save**. On release day, confirm the page changed to live destinations before saying **Listen now**.
- Add YouTube, YouTube Music, Tidal, Apple Music, or other DSP buttons only after their exact track URLs are verified. Do not add artist URLs in their place.
- Build a calendar using varied angles: announcement, hook/performance, story behind the song, second hook, countdown, release day, and after-release moment. Log each planned and published item so it is never posted twice.
- Use the platform-native Story link sticker for stories. Keep social videos platform-ready (typically a 9:16 vertical master) and preserve a clean source file.
- Review profiles, names, bios, profile images and social links before a release cycle; a bio is a profile change, not a claim of editorial endorsement.

## 5. Advertising

- Decide the funnel first: pre-save/site visits before release; streams, video views, followers, or retargeting after release. Do not activate a campaign merely because a draft is prepared.
- For ChatGPT Ads, prepare a campaign brief before opening Ads Manager: objective, markets, artist-approved audience, confirmed destination, campaign period, creative, budget proposal, measurement plan, and an approval checklist. The brief is not an ad buy and must not be presented as a live Ads Manager configuration.
- Review creative, destination, objective, countries, language, age, interests, placement, budget, date range, payment source, and estimated spend as separate fields.
- Broad targeting may be useful for algorithmic learning, but location and audience choices control who can realistically see an ad. Ads do not reach every person in a selected country.
- ChatGPT Ads, Spotify, Meta, YouTube, TikTok and other platforms have different availability, creative, billing, targeting and measurement rules. Re-check the relevant official source and live UI before advising on exact specs, pricing, country access, or total cost.
- Do not attach a payment method, approve a charge, or click an activation/publish button without express approval immediately beforehand.

## 6. Press and radio

1. Prepare a short pitch, a one-page factsheet, a press release, approved private listening link, and only rights-cleared images.
2. Find each current contact on the outlet's own official site. Do not use a generic inbox when a music desk, editorial form, or current named contact is available.
3. Check the Sent folder and the release contact tracker for that recipient, title and private link. This prevents duplicate pitches.
4. Send an individually tailored message. Lead with the local, prior-coverage, or editorial-fit reason; include release date, length, short description, private listening link and one clear request.
5. Do not attach a WAV master to unsolicited email. Offer WAV, metadata and images on request. Do not share images if permissions or photographer credits are unresolved.
6. Record `sent`, the precise date, recipient, subject/pitch type, and a single follow-up date five to seven working days later. Record bounces and replies too.

Urørt is a public-facing submission route, not a private pre-release storage area. Confirm whether its current form offers scheduling; if it does not, do not save a new song there before the intended public date unless the user clearly wants immediate public availability.

## 7. Release-day and post-release control

- Verify at least the primary DSP link, smart-link conversion, artist-page display, and any time-sensitive Canvas/lyrics state before announcing.
- Do not repost an announcement that is already live or scheduled. Check the native platform calendar/content list first.
- Monitor replies from press/radio and answer requests with the approved materials. A single useful follow-up is enough unless the recipient engages.
- Capture learnings: which asset performed best, audience/country response, editorial/radio outcomes, link conversion, and what must change for the next release.

## Minimum completion check

Before calling a release phase complete, identify the proof: a saved distributor screen, public live page, sent-message record, scheduled-content entry, successful link test, or explicit user confirmation. If proof is missing, call the item `unverified`, not `done`.
